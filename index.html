Daily detail

Forecast water temperature and salinity, the resulting index, and the modelled chance that conditions fall below the stress threshold.

How to read this. The index combines water temperature and salinity into a single measure of expected clam stress. A value near or above 1.0 means both are within the range clams tolerate well. Below 0.30 indicates likely stress, and sustained periods below that level are associated with reduced condition and possible mortality. Because forecasts tend to understate extremes, an amber “approaching stress” warning is raised before the index reaches 0.30 — testing against past events showed that waiting for 0.30 alone would miss about half of them.

Limitations. These are model forecasts, not measurements. Salinity in particular is forecast with limited accuracy, and the index is most uncertain during prolonged temperature extremes. Treat the stated probability, rather than the single number, as the guide. Not a substitute for on-site observation.

Data. Built from public National Weather Service, NOAA tide gauge, and U.S. Geological Survey river observations and forecasts. Updated hourly.
